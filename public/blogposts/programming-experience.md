# Programming experience (so far)

*October 17, 2024*

Well, as I mentioned in one of my previous blog posts, I've been programming for quite some time now. I will go more in depth here though *(STORY TIMEEE WOOO)*.

It all began one beautiful spring day during spring break. My father was, as per usual, watching some TV channel where, during one of their programs, they invite successful Bulgarians to tell their stories and how they succeeded in life. Well, that day, they had invited **Svetlin Nakov**. Most of you might not know him, but he's the CEO and co-founder of **SoftUni**.

> "But WTF is that???" you might say.

Well, *SoftUni*, hmm... 🤔 oh yeah! It must be *Software University*. "Yeah, but how does it work?" Well, **SoftUni** is like an online bootcamp selling online courses. Anyways, since we cleared this out, I can continue. So, as per usual, I was playing some videogame (I don't even remember which one at this point), and my father barged into my room telling me to come watch something with him (he always does that and never tells me what). So what do I do? I join him. We watch the whole interview, and my father asks me whether I would like to learn how to code. I wasn't quite sure, but I decided to give it a try. And so I did.

Now comes the question though... What language do I pick? So, after a quick YouTube montage of the *coolest programming languages* later, I had my choice ready. I was going to become the best **C#** developer. "Yeah, but why C#?" *Good question*. **VIDEOGAMES**. I wanted to create videogames, and that just happened to be the language the **Unity** game engine uses. So I enrolled in my first course: "Programming Basics with C#". I passed it with a **6.00/6.00**. My father encouraged me to pursue this since he saw my potential. And I did. I enrolled in the next course, "Programming Fundamentals with C#". You might be asking now:

> "Is he really going to just go through all of the courses he passed one by one?"

**No**. This course is where it all changed. It was during the summer. What do 14-15 year old teenagers do during the summer? They spend their time outside or in front of their computers, leaving no time for studying. I was no exception. I watched the first 2 lectures, then I started falling behind since I wasn't attending the lectures... and I **failed** the course 😔. I had convinced myself that I didn’t want to learn this anymore—not because I didn’t like it, but because I was just *too lazy* to catch up on my missed course lectures.

> "What did you do?"

Weeeeeeell.... at the time, I was coding on my insanely good (for the time) **Thinkpad**. I had saved up some money and had only one goal: *to buy myself a personal computer*. My console was no longer cutting it for me since all my friends could talk on **Discord** and I was ALONE. So I talked it through with my father aaaand he said that I could only get a PC if I continued with programming and this time, *pass* my exam for the course. I agreed. I learned all the material in a month instead of the provided 3 (cuz I’m that smart, yes), and... I didn’t get my PC (I know, it sucks).

> "Wtf, why did your dad screw you over like that?"

He didn't. **COVID** did. Everyone was at home and using their PCs or buying new ones, aaaaand the **GPU prices skyrocketed**.

Anyways, I continued to learn all about **SQL** and databases, **.NET**, and **EntityFramework**. After that, I studied **JavaScript** and created multiple webapps with **Node**, **Express**, **Page.js**, and **Handlebars**. Then I learned **Angular** and **MongoDB**, aaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaand I learned **HTML & CSS**(since I had just been winging it up until then 😅). After that, I picked up **React**, creating multiple projects with it. And this brings us to today. I’m at university, feeling kind of *overqualified* and waiting for something new to learn. Luckily, my favorite diva, **Frans Blauw**, is also my lecturer in Computer Science, where I’ve learned a bunch of new things.
